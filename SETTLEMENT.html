HOW THE SETTLEMENT WORKS

A city is not one object. It is a network of systems that must survive together.

01UNDERGROUND HABITAT

Modular living spaces are placed within lava-tube environments, using the Martian subsurface as part of the settlement's protective architecture.

02CORE & EXPANSION

The core arrives from Earth with essential equipment and 3D-printing robots. New modules expand outward from this starting point.

03WATER SYSTEM

Martian ice is extracted and transported through drilling, pipelines or tankers, while wastewater is recycled to reduce dependence on new resources.

04ENERGY NETWORK

Orbital solar collection and microwave transmission provide the primary energy pathway, supported by a fusion system for redundancy.

05SURFACE INFRASTRUCTURE

Communication, research, vehicle, energy and port facilities form the surface layer connecting the underground city with Mars and orbit.

06ROBOTS

Robotic systems support construction, infrastructure maintenance, agriculture, resource management, safety, emergency response and exploration.

07COMMUNITY

Residential, research, agricultural and civic functions are designed as one connected settlement rather than isolated technical modules.

08RESILIENCE

Redundancy, isolation, modularity, expandability and safety guide the architecture so that local failures do not become settlement-wide failures.

A CITY FOR PEOPLE

Survival is only the beginning.

A settlement is not successful simply because people can survive in it. They should be able to live with dignity, form relationships, find privacy, share responsibilities, celebrate, disagree, recover from failure, and feel that the place belongs to them. Designing a space settlement made me realize that engineering life-support systems is only half of the problem. The other half is designing a community worth sustaining.
