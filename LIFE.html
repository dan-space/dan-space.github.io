MISSION 05 / LIFE

A settlement needs more than systems. It needs a life worth living.

I began by designing habitats, energy networks, transportation, and water systems. But a city is not alive because its systems work. It becomes alive when people compete, lead, communicate, perform, serve, collaborate, challenge themselves, and explore. So before imagining life in a space settlement, I looked at the life I already know.

WHAT I BRING INTO THE CITY

A settlement grows through its systems. I grow through the experiences that teach me how to live, work, compete, and build with others. Each experience adds something I would bring into the city.

EIGHT THINGS A FUTURE CITY SHOULD PRESERVE

I found the answer in ordinary human experiences: challenge, responsibility, expression, art, service, collaboration, growth, and exploration.

BEYOND THE SKILL POINTS

Some of the systems I value most cannot be engineered: trust, friendship, humor, and the feeling of being part of a team. Badminton taught me to compete; collaborative projects taught me something harder—how to depend on other people.

01 / BADMINTON

Learning to respond before I have time to hesitate.

School team · weekly training · years of repetition

A MOMENT THAT STAYED WITH ME

At first, I thought badminton was about speed.

What kept me training was something more difficult: the gap between seeing what was coming and being ready to respond. A rally does not wait for a perfect plan. I learned to read patterns, commit quickly, and reset after mistakes instead of carrying them into the next point.

WHAT IT TAUGHT MEPreparation matters. So does the ability to begin again immediately.
